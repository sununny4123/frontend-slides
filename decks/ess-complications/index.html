Overview/02

Key Points

  • Primary surgery for chronic rhinosinusitis is almost exclusively performed endoscopically.
  • Functional endoscopic sinus surgery (FESS) aims to restore mucociliary function by reestablishing physiologic sinus ventilation and drainage.
  • Surgery should be personalized. The extent of surgery depends on symptoms and the pathology.
  • “Large” hole surgery may be employed in certain disease states to optimize topical drug delivery.
  • Identification of anatomic landmarks and variations helps limit complications.
  • Major complications of endoscopic sinus surgery (ESS) include cerebrospinal fluid leak, blindness, diplopia, internal carotid artery injury, and death.
  • ESS provides significant improvement in overall and disease-specific quality of life.
  • Common causes of failure of ESS include lateralized middle turbinate, failure to incorporate maxillary ostium in the middle meatal antrostomy, maxillary ostium stenosis, frontal recess scarring, residual ethmoidal air cells, and adhesions.

  • Radical

History & Evolution of ESS/06

From Caldwell-Luc to the Hopkins Rod

  • A surgical approach to the maxillary sinus through its anterior wall was described in 1675 by Molinetti.
  • In the 1890s, Caldwell, Spicer, and Luc, added an additional opening into the sinus through the inferior meatus under microscope and created the Caldwell-Luc procedures formed the primary surgical treatment of choice for chronic rhinosinusitis (CRS) through the early twentieth century.
  • Hirschmann conducted the first endoscopic examination of the nose with a modified cystoscope in 1901, examine the maxilla sinus through oroantral fistula
  • The modern era of endoscopic sinus surgery (ESS) evolved with the development of Hopkins rods in the 1950s.

History & Evolution of ESS/07

The Messerklinger Technique

  • Messerklinger pioneered the study of the endoscopic anatomy and pathophysiology of the paranasal sinuses and published his experience with ESS in 1978.
  • Technique involves an anterior-to-posterior approach, wherever two mucosal layer contact > disrupting MCC>retention secretion > infection, most likely to occur in narrow mucosal of MM and ethmoid air cell system.
  • Endoscope instead of external approach and microscope in sinus surgery
  • zero-degree scope
  • Removal of the uncinate process to expose the infundibulum.
  • Removal of the ethmoidal bulla, exposure of the frontal sinus ostium
  • Identification of the roof of the ethmoid and removal of the remaining anterior ethmoidal cells
  • Removal of the posterior ethmoidal cells
  • Finally, opening of the sphenoid sinus
  • The ostium of the maxillary sinus is then identified with use of a 30-degree endoscope and is widened.
Cumming Ed 7, Primary Sinus Surgery
History & Evolution of ESS/08

The Wigand Technique

  • Wigand ME 1978 divergence from the Messerklinger technique is the posterior-to-anterior approach for completing the ethmoidectomy.
  • The radical operations lead to scar formation and nerve irritation, A new concept is based on reestablishment of paranasal drainage, reventilation and preservation of the lining mucosa.
  • Begins with partial resection of the middle turbinate, opening of the posterior ethmoidal cells.
  • Removal of the anterior wall of the sphenoid sinus
  • The skull base is identified within the sphenoid sinus, dissection is continued anteriorly through the posterior and anterior ethmoids
  • The major advantage of this technique is early exposure of the skull base to reduce intraoperative CSF leak.
  • In practice, a combination of these techniques is typically used.

History & Evolution of ESS/09

Kennedy & FESS in the United States

  • Kennedy is credited with the introduction of Funtional Endoscopic Sinus Surgery to the United States in 1985. Anterior to posterior approach
  • The purpose of functional endoscopic sinus surgery is to reestablish ventilation and mucociliary clearance of the sinuses, correction of anatomical and inflammatory abnormalities that disturb MCC, removal of the chronic osteitic bone, preservation of sinonasal mucosa, widening of natural ostium
  • Endoscopic removal of disease from key areas of the anterior ethmoid and middle meatus.
  • The importance of disease in the ostiomeatal unit has previously been recognized as a major factor in the pathogenesis of sinusitis of the maxillary and frontal sinuses
  • The advantages - Minimize trauma to normal structures, the maxillary ostium is not opened circumferentially, removal of disease frontal recess rather than frontal ostium > effective period of treatment, as long as the internal os remains patent.
Kennedy DW. Functional Endoscopic Sinus Surgery, Technique. Arch Otolarygo 111: 576-582, 1985
History & Evolution of ESS/10

Minimally Invasive Sinus Technique (MIST)

  • Introduced in 1994, Minimally invasive sinus surgery (MIST)
  • A powered microdebrider with real time suction termed the “HUMMER,” was shown by Setliff and Parsons to be associated with accelerated healing and reduced synechia formation.
  • Provided anatomically based reproducible approach, Preservation of mucosa and turbinate tissue, Undisturbed the primary sinus ostium, Less involved posterior ethmoid and/or sphenoid cavities
  • Avoids unnecessary disruption of normal mucosa while restoring a mucociliary clearance through the primary birth ostia.
Catalano PJ. The minimal invasive sinus technique: Concepts and controversies. Operative Techniques in Otolaryngology, 2016

History & Evolution of ESS/12

Balloon Sinusotomy

  • Balloon Sinusotomy, 2006 Bolger WE
  • Dilation of the natural sinus ostium by a balloon inflation device
  • Balloon catheter dilatation may be in conjunction with conventional sinus surgery, the so-called “hybrid procedure.”
  • Insertion of a balloon dilation catheter over a guidewire that has been positioned within the involved paranasal sinus under endoscopic visualization, image guidance, or a light-assisted guidewire. Inflation of the balloon results in dilation of the targeted ostium.
  • Frontal sinus, Sphenoid sinus, Maxillary sinus, Anterior ethmoid sinus
  • A recent systematic review and meta-analysis : long-term improvement in quality-of-life and sinus opacification scores in CRS.
  • Adverse effects much like with conventional ESS includes orbital injury, CSF leak, and death

History & Evolution of ESS/13

Full-House FESS & the Reboot Approach

  • 2011 Full house FEES, Fully clear the entire paranasal sinuses, Complete large maxillary antrostomy, total ethmoidectomy, wide sphenoidotomy, and Draf IIA frontal sinusotomy, preserve mucosa and middle turbinate
  • Important goal is to increase access of the inflamed mucosa to anti-inflammatory topical therapies.
  • Reboot approach 2016
  • The CRSwNP type 2 inflammation and its associated deficiencies in defense against bacteria and viruses as well as epithelial repair.
  • The concept of reboot surgery, which is based on the removal of all inflamed sinus mucosa and allowing the regrowth of functional nasal mucosa.
  • The removal of all sinus mucosa could potentially cause problems during the healing process and lead to prolonged healing and scarring due to the full-thickness mucosal injury
Alsharif S. et al. Endoscopic Sinus Surgery for Type-2 CRS wNP: An Endotype-Based Retrospective Study.Laryngoscope 00: 2019
History & Evolution of ESS/14

Reboot Approach — Rationale & Outcomes

  • Only the complete suppression of type-2 inflammation may allow for a normal wound-healing process postoperatively, with new mucosa growing from the nasal cavity into the sinuses.
  • Patients with a type-2 sinus mucosa inflammation with polyp formation in two-thirds of subjects with prior surgery and comorbid asthma suggests that the reboot approach may offer a better outcome in severely ill CRSwNP than the conventional ESS without increasing the risk for intra or postoperative problems or complications.
Alsharif S. et al. Endoscopic Sinus Surgery for Type-2 CRS wNP: An Endotype-Based Retrospective Study.Laryngoscope 00: 2019

Principles of Functional Endoscopic Sinus Surgery

  • Decrease inflammatory burden
  • Prepare the nasal cavity for topical medications

Radiographic Assessment

  • Noncontrast CT scans: for preoperative planning and used for image guidance
  • Patients with CRS with positive endoscopy should be treated medically and the sinus CT obtained 3-4 wks after therapy to evaluate the effect of treatment
  • Specific variations that may predispose a surgeon to inadvertent penetration of the orbit or the anterior cranial cavity
  • (1) lamina papyracea lying medial to the maxillary ostium
  • (2) maxillary sinus hypoplasia
  • (3) fovea ethmoidalis abnormalities, such as a low or sloping fovea
  • (4) lamina papyracea dehiscence
  • (5) sphenoid sinus wall variations, such as septa attached to the carotid or dehiscence of the carotid or optic nerve
  • (6) SECs, Hyperpneumatization of the frontal sinus or presence of a frontal cell may be associated with a deep fovea ethmoidalis

Positioning & Anaesthesia/33

Choice of Anesthesia: LA vs GA

  • Limitations of LA :
  • 1. Patient anxiety and sudden patient movement
  • 2. Long period surgery 1 and 2 hours. Patients have difficulty remaining still
  • 3. Difficulty appropriate anesthesia
  • 4. Difficulty dealing with water, secretion,blood trickling into the pharynx, aspiration
  • 5. Difficult for teaching of residents
  • LA offered to patients having limited ESS confined to the middle meatus
  • GA : mean arterial pressure (MAP) of 55 to 70 mm Hg and heart rate under 60 beats per minute
  • TIVA has been proposed as a preferred anesthetic in ESS, because it is thought that TIVA decreases cardiac output without the significant decrease in systemic vascular resistance
Endoscopic Sinus Surgery, Peter-John WormaldCumming Ed 7, Primary Sinus Surgery

Recommendations for Topical Vasoconstrictor Use

  • 7 mg/kg, up to 500 mg
  • 1. Do not use topical phenylephrine if possible.
  • 2. Use caution with topical cocaine.
  • 3. Avoid b-blockers for intraoperative hypertension after topical vasoconstrictor use.
  • 4. Avoid halogenated hydrocarbon anesthetic agents (i.e., Halothane) when using topical vasoconstrictors.
  • 5. If possible, avoid using concentrated topical cocaine or epinephrine in patients with history of cardiovascular disease.
  • 6. For neonate to 85 pounds or 12-year-old child: consider using 0.05% oxymetalozine first. If adequate visualization or hemostasis is not achieved then consider 1:2,000 topical epinephrine with judicious use.
  • 7. For 85 pounds to 17 years of age: use oxymetalozine or 1:2,000 epinephrine with judicious use.
  • 8. 18 years or older: use 1:2,000 or 1;1,000 epinephrine with judicious use.

Injection: MT Axilla & Greater Palatine Block

  • The axilla of the middle turbinate is injected for anterior sinus surgery with approximately 1 to 1.5 mL of lidocaine 1% with epinephrine 1:100,000 until blanching.

Concluding & Postoperative Care/89

Concluding the Procedure

  • The sinonasal cavity is thoroughly irrigated, small bone fragments are removed, and hemostasis is ascertained.
  • Hot saline irrigations (49oC) improving surgical field of view in ESS after 2 hours of operating time, significant reduction blood loss.
  • Nondissolvable packs in the middle meatus are left in place for 3 to 7 days. Inferior packing in the nasal cavity, if used, can usually be removed before the patient is discharged or in 24 to 72 hours, greatly improving patient comfort
  • Suture on a Keith needle is passed through the right middle turbinate and septum. The needle is grabbed medial to the left middle turbinate.

  • Small defects <10 mm are closed by either placing an inlay of graft or merely “plugging” the defect with tissue, overlay and underlay grafting, and fibrin glue is useful in sealing and healing.
  • Large defects bone or cartilage is used to bridge the gap and prevent encephalocele formation
Cumming Ed 7, Primary Sinus SurgeryOperative Techniques in Otolaryngology, Vol 22, No 3, September 2011
